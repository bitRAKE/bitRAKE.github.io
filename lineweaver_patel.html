About This Diagram

An interactive exploration of the mass-radius diagram from "All objects and some questions" by Charles H. Lineweaver and Vihan M. Patel (2023), Research School of Astronomy & Astrophysics, Australian National University.

The original plot places every known composite object in the Universe on a log-log graph of mass vs. radius, revealing the forbidden regions imposed by general relativity (Schwarzschild black holes) and quantum mechanics (Compton wavelength).

Key Physics

Black hole line — Schwarzschild radius: rs = 2Gm/c². Objects cannot be smaller than their Schwarzschild radius; the region to the left is "forbidden by gravity."

Compton line — Compton wavelength: λc = ℏ/mc. Below this scale, quantum uncertainty prevents localization of a single particle.

Instanton — Where the two boundaries cross: a Planck-mass black hole. The smallest possible object.

Isodensity lines — Diagonal lines (slope 3) connecting objects of equal density, labeled by the epoch when the Universe had that density.

Density bands — Background shading shows density regimes from Planck-era through present-day matter density.
